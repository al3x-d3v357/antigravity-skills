# Job Description Optimizer

Audit and rewrite job descriptions so they attract A-players by making them excited AND helping them self-select accurately. Most JDs fail because they're written for the legal department, not the candidate.

## Process

```
1. AUDIT    -> Score the original JD against the failure checklist below
2. FLAG     -> List every issue found before rewriting
3. REWRITE  -> Apply the 5-section framework
4. CHECK    -> Verify the rewrite passes every rule
5. DELIVER  -> Audit summary + rewritten JD + changelog
```

## Phase 1: JD Audit

Before rewriting, flag every instance of these failures:

| Failure | Example | Why It's Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement inflation | "5–7 years experience in X" | Deters qualified candidates, especially women |
| Task list vs. outcomes | "Responsible for managing social media" | Tells them what to do, not why it matters |
| Generic company description | "Fast-paced, innovative company" | Meaningless — every company says this |
| Salary opacity | No range listed | Top candidates skip opaque postings |
| Buzzword culture signals | "Rock star", "ninja", "guru" | Signals low-quality culture |
| Kitchen sink requirements | 20+ requirements for a mid-level role | Self-selects out qualified candidates |
| Legal compliance issues | Age-discriminatory language, protected class signals | Flag immediately — do not silently remove |

**Output the audit before the rewrite:**
```
## JD Audit: [Role Title]
❌ [Issue 1] — [Where it appears] — [Why it's a problem]
❌ [Issue 2] — ...
⚠️ [Legal risk] — [Exact language] — [Recommended fix]
```

## Phase 2: Rewrite Framework

### Section 1: The Hook (First 50 Words — Most Important)
Candidates read the first paragraph and decide whether to keep reading. It must:
- State the most compelling thing about this opportunity
- Signal the type of person who thrives here
- Create excitement without being generic

❌ "We're looking for someone to join our growing team."
✅ "We're hiring our first [Role] because [specific challenge]. You'll own [specific impact area] from day one."

### Section 2: Why This Role Exists
```
We're hiring a [Role] because [specific business challenge or opportunity].
The person in this role will [specific measurable impact].
```

### Section 3: What You'll Do (Outcomes, Not Tasks)
Lead each bullet with the outcome, not the activity:

❌ "Manage our content calendar"
✅ "Own our content strategy end to end — from editorial calendar to distribution — and grow organic traffic"

❌ "Collaborate with engineering"
✅ "Partner with engineering to ship features as the bridge between user needs and technical constraints"

### Section 4: What Makes You Right For This
Separate Must Haves from Nice to Haves. Hard cap: **5 must-haves maximum.**

Use behaviors and outcomes, not credentials:
❌ "5+ years management experience"
✅ "Has hired, onboarded, and developed a team of 3+ people"

❌ "Strong communication skills"
✅ "Can turn a complex technical decision into a one-page brief a CEO can act on"

Never gatekeep on years of experience alone.

### Section 5: What You'll Get
Be specific on every item — never say "competitive":
- **Salary**: Always include a range
- **Equity**: Range + what it represents at current valuation (if applicable)
- **Benefits**: Name them specifically
- **Career path**: What does this role naturally lead to in 18–24 months?
- **Working norms**: Remote/hybrid policy, meeting cadence, how decisions get made — actual culture signals, not platitudes

## Output Format

```
## JD Audit: [Role Title]
[Findings with severity]

---

## Rewritten Job Description
[Full rewrite using 5-section framework]

---

## What Changed and Why
[Changelog — what was removed, added, or reframed and the reason]
```

## Rules

- Always include salary range — non-negotiable
- Use "you" throughout — speak directly to the candidate
- Never use years of experience as the only qualifier for a requirement
- Maximum 5 must-have requirements
- Flag legal compliance issues explicitly — never silently remove them
- The JD should read like a conversation with a smart hiring manager, not a legal document

## How to Trigger

```
"Rewrite this JD to attract A-players. Lead with outcomes. Remove gatekeeping."
[Paste original JD]
```

Or from scratch:
```
"Write a job description for a [role] at a [stage] company.
The role exists because [reason]. Must-haves: [list].
Salary range: [X–Y]. Remote/hybrid/onsite: [policy]."
```

## Edge Cases

**Highly technical roles**: Don't oversimplify requirements — senior technical candidates want to see you understand the actual technical scope. Keep technical specifics, just frame as outcomes not credentials.

**Regulated industries with mandatory credentials**: Keep the legitimate credential requirements, apply outcome framing to everything else around them.

**Replacing a departing person**: Write for what you need going forward, not what you had. Ask "what problem does this role need to solve?" and write from there — don't let the previous person's profile define the requirements.
