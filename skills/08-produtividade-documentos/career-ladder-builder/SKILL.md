# Career Ladder Builder

Build career ladders that are honest, specific, and equitable — the kind employees actually use to understand what's expected and managers actually use to make objective promotion decisions.

## The 4 Core Principles

**1. Describe behavior, not traits**
Don't say "strong communicator." Say "communicates complex technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders clearly, in writing and verbally, without being asked to simplify."

**2. Describe scope of impact, not just skill**
The difference between levels is usually the SCOPE of impact, not just the sophistication of the skill: Individual → Team → Org → Company/Market.

**3. Be honest about the bar**
Calibrate to YOUR context. A Level 4 at your company might be a Level 2 at Google. That's fine — what matters is that the levels reflect real expectations at your stage.

**4. Separate IC and Management tracks at senior levels**
Don't force management on people who are excellent individual contributors. At Senior and above, offer both paths where the role supports it.

## Process

```
1. GATHER    -> Role title + company stage + any values or competencies to embed
2. ANCHOR    -> Define the Senior level first (what does excellence look like?) then work outward
3. BUILD     -> Write all 4 levels using the template below
4. FLAG      -> Mark any sections needing domain expert input with ⚠️ NEEDS EXPERT REVIEW
5. DELIVER   -> Full ladder + a one-page summary matrix at the end
```

## Career Ladder Template (Use for All 4 Levels)

```markdown
## Level [X]: [Title]
e.g., L1: Junior / L2: Mid / L3: Senior / L4: Staff or Lead

**Summary**: [2 sentences — who this person is and what they contribute]
**Scope of Impact**: [Individual / Team / Org / Company]
**Typical Time to Reach from Previous Level**: [Range, not guarantee — e.g., "12–24 months with demonstrated performance"]

---

### Technical / Functional Competencies

| Competency | What Level X looks like |
|---|---|
| [Core skill 1] | [Specific behavioral description] |
| [Core skill 2] | [Specific behavioral description] |
| [Core skill 3] | [Specific behavioral description] |

### Leadership & Collaboration
[How they work with and through others at this level]

### Communication
[How they communicate — to whom, in what situations, at what level of complexity]

### Judgment & Ownership
[How they handle ambiguity, setbacks, and decisions without being told what to do]

### What Level X Is NOT
[Common behaviors that LOOK like this level but don't meet the bar — prevents grade inflation]
```

## Summary Matrix (Always Include at the End)

After all 4 levels, produce a one-page matrix:

```markdown
## Career Ladder Summary: [Role Title]

| Dimension | L1: Junior | L2: Mid | L3: Senior | L4: Staff/Lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope of Impact | Individual | Team | Org | Company |
| [Competency 1] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] |
| [Competency 2] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] |
| Leadership | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] |
| Judgment | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] | [1-line] |
| Typical tenure to reach | Starting point | 12–18 mo | 18–36 mo | 36+ mo |
```

## Rules

- Every behavioral description must be specific enough that two different managers would independently agree on whether an employee meets it
- Always include "What this level is NOT" — this is what prevents grade inflation
- Never use "exceeds expectations" as a level descriptor without defining what the expectation is
- Company values should appear as behavioral descriptions in the ladder, not just mentioned — show what living that value looks like at each level

## How to Trigger

```
"Build a 4-level career ladder for [role] at a [stage] company.
Use behavioral descriptions at each level.
Our company values are [X, Y, Z] — make those visible in the ladder."
```

## Edge Cases

**Deeply technical roles (engineering, data, security)**: The technical competencies section requires input from senior practitioners. Mark any technically-specific behavioral descriptions with ⚠️ NEEDS EXPERT REVIEW rather than inventing them.

**Roles new to the company**: Start with the Senior level (what does excellent look like?) and work backward to Junior. Don't start at L1 — the anchor point should be the aspirational end state.

**Roles with natural ceilings**: Some roles (customer support, for example) don't have an infinite ladder. Design for the realistic scope of the role — 3 levels with honesty is better than 5 levels where L4 and L5 are fabricated distinctions.

**IC vs. Management split**: At L3/L4, if the role supports both tracks, produce two parallel versions of those levels — one for the IC path and one for the management path. Label them clearly: "L4: Senior Engineer (IC)" and "L4: Engineering Manager."
