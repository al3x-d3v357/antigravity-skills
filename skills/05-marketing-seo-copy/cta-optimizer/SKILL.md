## What I Do

Rewrite any call-to-action to reduce friction, increase perceived value, and match the ask to the relationship stage. I output 5 CTA variants across a friction spectrum with a recommended pick.

## The 4 Components of a Great CTA

1. **Low perceived effort** — What does it COST the reader? (time, commitment, risk)
2. **High perceived value** — What do they GET? (outcome, not feature)
3. **Specificity** — Vague CTAs get ignored; specific CTAs convert
4. **Right friction level** — Match the ask to the relationship stage

## Friction Level Guide

| Stage | Right CTA Friction | Wrong CTA Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Cold outreach | Reply with 1 word | Book a 30-min demo |
| Warm follow-up | Quick 15-min call | Sign contract / Make decision |
| Post-demo | Intro to stakeholder | "Let me know your thoughts" |
| Closing | Review proposal by Fri | "When you're ready..." |

## Process

```
1. DIAGNOSE  -> Identify the current CTA's weakness (too vague? too much friction? wrong stage?)
2. STAGE     -> Determine relationship stage + appropriate friction level
3. GENERATE  -> 5 variants across the friction spectrum
4. RECOMMEND -> Flag best variant with ✅ + 1-sentence reason
```

## The 5 Variants

- **Variant A**: Lowest friction possible
- **Variant B**: Slight increase, adds specificity
- **Variant C**: Balanced — best blend of value + specificity + friction ✅ usually here
- **Variant D**: Higher ask with stronger value justification
- **Variant E**: Time-anchored or urgency-based

## How to Trigger

```
"Optimize this CTA: [paste your CTA]
Relationship stage: [cold / warm / post-demo / closing]
The reader is a [Title] at a [type of company]."
```

## Rules

- Never use "Let me know what you think"
- Never use "Feel free to reach out"
- Never use open-ended asks with no clear action
- Always specify WHO does WHAT by WHEN (or a clear next step)

## Edge Cases

**B2C vs. B2B**: Lower friction for B2C (impulse-friendly). Higher value justification for B2B (committee-friendly).

**Automated sequences**: Automated CTAs should feel personal — flag where personalization tokens should go.

**No-reply objection**: If the CTA asks for something with no obvious reader benefit, flag this as a structural problem, not a copy problem.

## Example

**Input**: "CTA: 'Let me know if you'd like to chat.' Stage: warm follow-up. Reader: Marketing Director at a SaaS company."

**Output**:

```
Variant A (Lowest Friction)
"Reply 'yes' and I'll send over 3 times that work this week."

Variant B (Adds Specificity)
"Worth a 15-min call to show you how [Company] cut CAC 30%? I have Tuesday 2pm or Thursday 11am."

Variant C (Balanced) ✅
"Happy to walk you through how this works for SaaS teams your size — does Thursday afternoon work?"

Variant D (Higher Ask, Justified)
"I put together a short breakdown of what this could look like for [Company]. Want me to send it over before we talk?"

Variant E (Time-Anchored)
"I have one spot open this week for a 15-min walkthrough — want me to hold Thursday at 3pm?"

✅ Recommended: Variant C — specific enough to feel real, low enough friction for a warm lead, human enough to not feel automated.
```
